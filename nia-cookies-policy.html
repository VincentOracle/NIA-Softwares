1. What Are Cookies?

Cookies are small text files or similar browser technologies that websites may store on a visitor's device. They can help websites remember information, maintain functionality, understand how a website is used, or support other website features.

Not every website uses every type of cookie. The exact technologies in use depend on how a particular website is built and configured. Some cookies are set directly by the website, while others may be set by third-party services that the website relies on.

Cookies are not inherently harmful. They are a standard part of how many websites operate today.

2. How We Use Cookies

Where cookies or similar technologies are used on the NIA Softwares website, they may support purposes such as:

  • operating essential website functionality;
  • remembering certain preferences, where implemented;
  • understanding website usage, where analytics are implemented;
  • improving the website experience;
  • supporting security or technical functionality.

Where analytics tools are implemented, cookies or similar technologies may be used to understand how visitors interact with the website. NIA Softwares does not state that any particular analytics provider is in use unless such a service is actually implemented.

  • interface preferences;

  • form state, where implemented;
  • display preferences.

11. Managing Cookies

Visitors can generally manage cookies through their browser. Most modern browsers allow users to:

  • view cookies stored on their device;
  • delete cookies that have already been set;
  • block cookies from specific websites;
  • block cookies from all websites;
  • set preferences for certain types of cookies.

Browser settings vary, and specific instructions should be checked in the browser's own help documentation. Visitors may also use browser extensions or operating-system tools to manage cookies.

Where cookie preference controls are provided by the website, visitors can use those controls to manage applicable cookie categories. Visitors can also manage cookies through their browser settings.

12. What Happens When Cookies Are Disabled?

Blocking or deleting cookies may affect some website functionality, depending on which cookies are disabled. Possible impacts may include:

  • preferences may not be remembered;
  • certain interactive functions may not work as expected;
  • consent preferences may need to be selected again, where applicable.

Disabling cookies should not usually prevent access to the website's general public content, but the exact effect depends on the website's technical implementation.

13. Cookie Retention

Cookies generally fall into two broad categories based on how long they remain on a device:

  • Session cookies: typically removed when the browsing session ends.
  • Persistent cookies: remain on the device for a defined period or until deleted.

Specific retention periods depend on the cookie and its purpose. Retention periods should be confirmed against the website's current cookie inventory.
